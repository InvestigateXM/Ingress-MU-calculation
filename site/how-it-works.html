How cells and densities decide the Mind Units

The Method page explains exactly how the Mind Units (MU) of a field are calculated. This page shows the main ideas step by step, first on simple made-up examples and then on real example fields. The coordinates are example coordinates, not necessarily portals.

Words used on this page
Cell
The game divides the world into nested, roughly square cells. Each cell splits into four smaller cells (its quarters or children).
Level
The size of a cell. Higher levels are smaller: a level-13 cell, the smallest used for MU, is about 1 km²; a level-10 cell about 80 km².
Covering
The cells the game uses for one field: together they cover the whole field. A field uses at most 20 cells.
Density
How many MU one km² inside a cell is worth. It belongs to the whole cell, not to the spot where your field is.
Overlap
The part of the field that lies inside a cell, in km².
Contribution
The MU one cell gives a field before rounding: overlap × density.

1. The big picture

The game lays a grid of cells over your field. Each cell has a density: how many MU one km² in that cell is worth. The part of your field in each cell is multiplied by that cell's density, and the results are added up.

2. A field inside one cell

This small field lies inside a single smallest cell. Its MU is the area of the field times the density of the whole cell.

3. A smaller field with more MU

A field that lies completely inside another field can earn more MU, because the two fields can use different cells for the same ground.

4. Why cells have different sizes

Every cell splits into four smaller cells. The level of a cell says how often this has happened.

5. Big cells inside, small cells at the edge

The game prefers big cells where it can. When all four quarters of a cell are needed, it uses the whole cell instead. Inside the field that changes almost nothing; at the edge it can change the MU noticeably.

6. The budget of 20 cells

A field is described by at most 20 cells. A big field therefore gets big cells that stick out of the field, and each of them counts with the density of the whole cell.

7. Where the density of a cell comes from

The density of a cell is read from a population map of the year 2000. Inside each smallest cell the game places 16 evenly spaced measuring points, reads the map at each point and takes the mean.
